KapitelüberblickChapter overview

Kapitel 11 is shopping-focused: clothing vocabulary and the small but essential grammar pair welch- ("which") and dieser ("this"), which behave like twins across all three cases you already know.

What this chapter is about

You'll learn clothing items and colors, verbs for talking about how clothes look and fit (gefallen, stehen, aussehen), and the question word welch- ("which") together with the demonstrative dieser ("this") across nominative, accusative, and dative.

What you should be able to say after finishing it

  • Name clothing items and colors
  • Say what you like, what suits you, and what looks good
  • Ask "which one?" and answer "this one" in any case
  • Have a basic clothes-shopping conversation
  • Give an opinion about someone's outfit politely

Where you use this in real life

Shopping for clothes, asking a shop assistant which size or color is available, complimenting or asking Sonia's opinion on an outfit, or browsing online (Zalando, Amazon) and discussing options.

GrammatikGrammar

welch- and dieser both change their endings exactly like der/die/das across the cases — once you see the pattern, both words become easy.

1. welch- "which" — asks for a specific choice

CaseMasculineFeminineNeuterPlural
Nominativewelcherwelchewelcheswelche
Accusativewelchenwelchewelcheswelche
Dativewelchemwelcherwelchemwelchen
✓
Welcher Pullover gefällt dir?
Which sweater do you like? (nominative, masc.)
✓
Welche Jacke möchtest du?
Which jacket would you like? (accusative, fem. — no change from nom.)
✓
Mit welchem Anzug gehst du?
Which suit are you going with? (dative, masc., after mit)

2. dieser "this" — points to something specific, follows the same pattern

Accusativediesendiesediesesdiese

Welcher Rock? — Dieser hier.

✓

✓

Memory trick

welch- and dieser share the exact same set of endings — -er, -e, -es, -en, -em — laid over their different stems. If you know one, you effectively know both. They also mirror the der/die/das pattern you already learned, just with a different starting letter.

3. Verbs for talking about clothes

VerbMeaningExample
gefallento like (dative)Die Jacke gefällt mir.
stehento suit (dative)Das steht dir gut.
aussehento look (appearance)Du siehst toll aus.
passento fit (dative)Die Hose passt mir nicht.
anziehento put on (separable)Ich ziehe die Jacke an.

Common mistake

All four of gefallen, stehen, passen, and the object of admiration in gefallen take a dative person — this is the same dative-verb pattern from Kapitel 7, now applied to shopping. Das gefällt ich is wrong; it must be Das gefällt mir.

Häufige FehlerCommon mistakes

Das gefällt ich.
Das gefällt mir.
Why: gefallen always takes a dative pronoun for the person doing the liking, never a nominative subject pronoun.
Welcher Jacke gefällt dir?
Welche Jacke gefällt dir?
Why: die Jacke is feminine, so welch- must take its feminine ending (welche), matching der/die/das exactly.
Die Hose passt ich nicht.
Die Hose passt mir nicht.
Why: passen, like gefallen and stehen, is a fixed dative verb — the person is always the dative object.
