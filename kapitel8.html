KapitelüberblickChapter overview

Kapitel 8 extends the imperative (command form) you learned in Kapitel 3 to du and ihr — the forms you'll actually use with friends, family, or a doctor giving you informal advice. It's paired with body-part and health vocabulary because commands and advice go hand in hand.

What this chapter is about

You'll learn body parts, common illnesses and symptoms, how to describe pain and injuries, basic vocabulary for a doctor's visit, and — grammatically — the du- and ihr-imperative forms, which let you give informal instructions and advice.

What you should be able to say after finishing it

  • Name body parts and say where something hurts
  • Describe basic symptoms to a doctor or pharmacist
  • Give informal commands and advice to a friend
  • Understand simple health instructions and recommendations
  • Talk about fitness and exercise habits

Where you use this in real life

Describing an ache to a doctor or pharmacist, telling a friend to rest or drink water when they're sick, following basic health instructions in German, or discussing fitness routines.

GrammatikGrammar

The main new grammar: du- and ihr-imperative forms, completing the command system you started with Sie in Kapitel 3.

1. The du-imperative informal singular commands

To give an informal command to one person, drop the -st ending from the du-form and often drop the -e from the stem too (though keeping it is also fine and common).

du-form minus -st → imperative (no "du" spoken)
Infinitivedu-formdu-imperative
trinkendu trinkstTrink!
essendu isstIss!
schlafendu schläfstSchlaf!
nehmendu nimmstNimm!
✓
Trink viel Wasser!
Drink lots of water!
✓
Nimm die Tablette!
Take the pill!
✕
Du trink viel Wasser!
Wrong — the du-imperative drops "du" entirely; you never say it aloud

Common mistake

Verbs that change their vowel in the du-form (like nehmen → nimmst, essen → isst) keep that vowel change in the imperative too: Nimm!, Iss! — not Nehm! or Ess!. This is a frequent A1 error because it looks like it should go back to the infinitive stem.

2. The ihr-imperative informal plural commands

For a group of people you'd address as ihr, simply use the ihr-form of the verb and drop the pronoun.

Infinitiveihr-formihr-imperative
trinkenihr trinktTrinkt!
essenihr esstEsst!
✓
Kommt bitte pünktlich!
Please come on time! (to a group of friends)

3. Summary — all three imperative forms together

FormUsed fortrinkennehmen
Sieformal, any numberTrinken Sie!Nehmen Sie!
duinformal, one personTrink!Nimm!
ihrinformal, groupTrinkt!Nehmt!

Memory trick

Only the Sie-imperative keeps its pronoun and flips word order (verb-Sie). Both du- and ihr-imperatives simply drop the pronoun from the normal present-tense form — no word order change needed, just delete "du"/"ihr" and, for du, usually the -st ending.

4. Saying where something hurts weh tun

[body part, dative if pronoun] + tut/tun + [dative person] + weh.
✓
Mein Kopf tut weh.
My head hurts.
✓
Mir tut der Bauch weh.
My stomach hurts. (literally: "to me the stomach hurts")
✓
Meine Beine tun weh.
My legs hurt. (plural: tun, not tut)

Häufige FehlerCommon mistakes

Du trink viel Wasser!
Trink viel Wasser!
Why: the imperative drops the pronoun entirely — saying "du" out loud in a command sounds unnatural and defeats the purpose of the imperative form.
Nehm die Tablette!
Nimm die Tablette!
Why: verbs with a stem-vowel change in the du-form (nehmen → nimmst) keep that change in the imperative — it doesn't revert to the plain infinitive stem.
Mein Kopf tun weh.
Mein Kopf tut weh.
Why: Kopf is singular, so the verb must agree — tut, not the plural tun.
