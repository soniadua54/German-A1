Zeit mit Freunden — Time with friends

Making plans, dates, weather, and talking about the past with war/hatte

KapitelüberblickChapter overview

Kapitel 6 gives you your first taste of talking about the past — but only for two verbs. This is deliberate: German uses a special simple-past form for haben and sein even in casual speech, while almost every other verb uses a different past tense you'll learn later. Learn these two now; they're used constantly.

What this chapter is about

You'll learn to make plans with friends, talk about dates and birthdays, describe the weather, and use the simple past (Präteritum) forms of haben and sein — hatte and war — to talk about how your weekend was.

What you should be able to say after finishing it

  • Suggest meeting up and agree on a time and place
  • Say the date and wish someone happy birthday
  • Describe the weather
  • Say how your weekend was, using war and hatte
  • Apologize for being late and respond graciously when someone apologizes to you

Where you use this in real life

Arranging a weekend meetup, small talk about the weather (a genuine German conversational staple), wishing a colleague happy birthday, or the classic Monday-morning "how was your weekend?" exchange.

GrammatikGrammar

The headline grammar point: the simple past of haben and sein. Also covered: dates with am, and weather expressions using es.

1. Präteritum of sein "was/were" — war

PronounForm
ichwar
duwarst
er/sie/eswar
wirwaren
✓
Wie war dein Wochenende? — Es war sehr schön.
How was your weekend? — It was very nice.
✓
Das Wetter war super.
The weather was great.
✕
Das Wetter ist super gewesen letztes Wochenende.
Overcomplicated — for sein, native speakers default to the simple war, not the compound perfect tense

2. Präteritum of haben "had" — hatte

PronounForm
ichhatte
duhattest
er/sie/eshatte
wirhatten
✓
Ich hatte Geburtstag am Samstag.
I had my birthday on Saturday. (German literally says "had birthday")
✓
Hattest du Spaß?
Did you have fun?

Memory trick

war and hatte are the two exceptions to the "learn Perfekt for the past" rule you'll meet in later chapters. Even fluent speakers say ich war and ich hatte, never the clunkier compound forms, in everyday conversation. Learn these two now as fixed, frequently-used words.

Learn later

Every other verb's past tense (Perfekt, with haben/sein + past participle — e.g. ich habe gespielt) comes in a later chapter. For now, only war and hatte are yours to use freely.

3. Dates with am saying "on the ___th"

am + [ordinal number] + [month]
✓
Mein Geburtstag ist am zwölften Juni.
My birthday is on the 12th of June.
✓
Wir treffen uns am ersten Mai.
We're meeting on the 1st of May.

Learn later

The full system for forming ordinal numbers (erster, zweiter, dritter...) is grammar-heavy — for A1, memorize the dates you personally need (your birthday, key appointments) as fixed chunks rather than trying to derive every ordinal number from a rule yet.

4. Talking about weather using the impersonal "es"

Weather in German is expressed with the impersonal subject es ("it") — similar to English "it's raining."

✓
Es regnet.
It's raining.
✓
Es ist warm/kalt/sonnig.
It's warm/cold/sunny.
✓
Wie war das Wetter? — Es war sonnig und warm.
How was the weather? — It was sunny and warm.

SprechfabrikSpeaking factory

Dialogue 1 — Monday morning small talk

KOLLEGIN
Wie war dein Wochenende?
How was your weekend?
AMAN
Gut, danke! Ich hatte Geburtstag und war mit Freunden in der Strandbar.
Good, thanks! I had my birthday and was with friends at the beach bar.
KOLLEGIN
Oh, du hattest Geburtstag? Herzlichen Glückwunsch!
Oh, it was your birthday? Congratulations!
AMAN
Danke schön! Und das Wetter war super.
Thank you! And the weather was great.

Dialogue 2 — Making plans

LISA
Wollen wir am Samstag etwas machen?
Do we want to do something on Saturday?
AMAN
Ja, gern! Wollen wir ins Kino gehen?
Yes, gladly! Do we want to go to the cinema?
LISA
Gute Idee. Um wie viel Uhr?
Good idea. What time?
AMAN
Um sieben. Treffpunkt am Bahnhof?
At seven. Meeting point at the station?
LISA
Perfekt, bis Samstag!
Perfect, see you Saturday!

Dialogue 3 — Apologizing for being late

AMAN
Entschuldigung, ich bin spät. Der Bus war spät.
Sorry, I'm late. The bus was late.
SONIA
Kein Problem. Das nächste Mal bitte pünktlich!
No problem. Please be on time next time!

AussprachePronunciation

w (war)
Pronounced "v," as established in Kapitel 1 — worth reinforcing here since war/warst/waren appear constantly.
war · warst · waren
ü (Glückwunsch)
Round lips, say "i" — appears in this very common congratulatory phrase.
Glückwunsch
pünktlich
ü + a crisp final -lich (like "likh," soft ch after i/e sounds).
pünktlich

Häufige FehlerCommon mistakes

Das Wetter ist gut gewesen.
Das Wetter war gut.
Why: native speakers default to the simple past war for sein, even in speech — the compound form, while not wrong, sounds unnecessarily formal here.
Du war spät.
Du warst spät.
Why: war only goes with ich/er/sie/es — du takes warst, a different ending entirely.
Ich bin Geburtstag gehabt.
Ich hatte Geburtstag.
Why: this mixes up two different verb systems — use hatte, the simple past of haben, not a compound form built with sein.
